THE PROBLEM

You already know your market. It's the words that let you down.

Every agent has had the same week: the expired listing you should have won, the commission you discounted because you were put on the spot, the price reduction conversation you postponed for six weeks.

I froze when they said the fee was too high. And then you cut it — because in that moment you had no other answer. That discount cost you more than the fee did.
The other agent told them a bigger number, so I lost the listing. You were right and they were beaten by a flattering figure. Knowing you were right isn't worth much at the closing table.
I know it's overpriced and I can't bring myself to say it. So you list at their number, and then spend nine weeks arriving at the conversation you could have had on day one.
I'll call my past clients when things are quiet. You won't, and neither does anyone else. Ninety per cent of agents go silent after closing — which is why the ones who don't eat first.
THE FIX

Stop improvising the conversations that pay you.

These are not theory. Each script is structured around one thing: getting the other person the accurate information they need, faster — so the decision becomes easy. Every one is written to be said out loud, with the exact words, why they work, and what to say when the conversation turns.

Will this work in my market?

The scripts are written for US transactions and US rules — buyer agreements, MLS, inspection contingencies. The structure transfers; the placeholders ([Street], [Brokerage], your fee) are yours to fill. If you're outside the US, the fair-housing and do-not-call notes will not match your law, so check locally before using them.
